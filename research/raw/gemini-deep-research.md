Prepped: Technical Research and Architecture Report for an AI-Assisted Learning Platform1. Executive Summary and Strongest Product OpportunityThe development of Prepped, a zero-to-one AI-assisted learning platform, represents a structural shift in educational technology, moving from rigid, scripted interactions to fluid, real-time conversational practice grounded in evidence-based learning science. Current market offerings remain fragmented, forcing learners to choose between isolated pronunciation drills, generic open-ended AI chatbots, or static flashcard systems. The strongest product opportunity for Prepped lies in synthesizing ultra-low-latency conversational AI with dynamic state-machine-driven roleplay, automated Goodness of Pronunciation (GOP) acoustic scoring, and active recall powered by the modern Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS) algorithm.Technological advancements in the current landscape render this integration highly feasible and economically viable. The advent of ultra-low-latency text-to-speech (TTS) models, specifically Kokoro (an 82-million parameter model capable of sub-30-millisecond latency), combined with deterministic Language Processing Unit (LPU) inference via Groq, allows for natural, interruption-tolerant voice interactions that successfully mimic human conversational dynamics.By employing WebRTC via LiveKit for media transport, modeling conversation logic with finite state machines via XState, and grounding assessments in validated rubrics such as the IELTS band descriptors and ACTFL proficiency guidelines, the architecture provides a scientifically rigorous, technically unparalleled platform for language learning, interview preparation, and professional communication coaching.2. Learning Science and Assessment DesignThe core differentiator of the platform is its strict adherence to evidence-based learning science. Generic large language models cannot objectively grade pronunciation because they operate on text transcriptions, which inherently obscure acoustic variations. Similarly, naive daily reminders do not optimize memory. The platform must implement mathematically proven cognitive and acoustic models.Spaced Repetition: The Evolution to FSRSFor over three decades, the SuperMemo-2 (SM-2) algorithm, created by Piotr Woźniak in 1987, dominated the spaced repetition landscape. SM-2 relied on a static formula that multiplied intervals using a per-card ease factor. While revolutionary at the time, SM-2's core weakness is that it does not explicitly model the probability of forgetting; it merely applies a heuristic. This frequently leads to overly conservative intervals, forcing users into "ease hell" where they review easy cards too frequently, leading to burnout.The platform will bypass SM-2 entirely and implement the Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS), developed by Jarrett Ye. FSRS treats memory as a statistical prediction problem, answering the question: when does the probability of recalling an item drop to exactly 90%?. FSRS utilizes machine learning to fit curves across three explicit parameters, known as the DSR model:Difficulty (D): A per-card metric (scaled 1-10) representing the inherent complexity of the material for the specific learner.Stability (S): The time required for the probability of recall to decay from 100% to 90%.Retrievability (R): The calculated probability of successful recall on any given day.Extensive benchmarks conducted on over 500 million review logs demonstrate that FSRS achieves the targeted retention rate while requiring 20% to 30% fewer reviews than SM-2. By integrating the fsrs-rs-python binding, the backend can dynamically calculate optimum review intervals for vocabulary and grammar concepts seamlessly extracted from the user's conversational roleplays. It is noted that FSRS requires approximately 1,000 review data points to fully personalize its model to an individual; prior to this threshold, it utilizes highly accurate default parameters derived from global benchmarks.Acoustic Assessment: Goodness of Pronunciation (GOP)To provide objective phonetic feedback, the platform will implement a dedicated Goodness of Pronunciation (GOP) pipeline, a technique originally defined by Witt and Young in 2000. GOP calculates the duration-normalized log of the posterior probability that the speaker actually produced the expected phoneme, given the raw acoustic features of the audio.The traditional pipeline requires three steps:Expected Sounds: The target text is converted into a canonical phoneme sequence via a pronunciation dictionary.Forced Alignment: An automatic speech recognition (ASR) acoustic model identifies the exact start and end times of each phoneme within the user's audio buffer.Log Posterior Probability (LPP): The system calculates the ratio between the likelihood of the expected phoneme and the sum of likelihoods of all possible phonemes to generate a confidence score.While traditional forced alignment is effective, it struggles when learners insert extra sounds or delete phonemes entirely. Therefore, the platform will utilize an alignment-free Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) based GOP method. This approach considers all possible segmentations simultaneously, effectively handling substitution, deletion, and insertion errors without forcing an unnatural alignment onto mispronounced speech.Because single-phoneme GOP scoring correlates with human expert ratings at roughly 0.61 to 0.72 (compared to sentence-level correlation of 0.74 to 0.78), the user interface must present these scores contextually. Users will be directed to identify persistent patterns of errors (e.g., repeatedly failing the /θ/ to /f/ transition) rather than obsessing over a single low score on an isolated phoneme.The Shadowing TechniqueShadowing is an active, highly cognitive psycholinguistic technique where learners track and repeat heard speech with a minimal delay—typically around 150 milliseconds. Originally utilized in training simultaneous UN interpreters, shadowing forces the learner to mimic the speaker's speed, rhythm, stress, and intonation without pausing to analyze grammar.Research indicates that consistent shadowing practice leads to significant improvements in listening accuracy, speech rate, and suprasegmental pronunciation control (fluency and prosody). The platform will feature a dedicated Shadowing Mode where users listen to Kokoro-generated audio and speak simultaneously. The system will record the user, perform GOP analysis, and map the user's prosody (pitch and rhythm) against the generated baseline, providing visual feedback on intonation mismatches.Validated Assessment FrameworksTo provide objective, recognizable feedback, evaluations will be aligned with established international frameworks. Security, Privacy, Reliability, Accessibility, and Evaluation DesignDeploying AI systems in an educational context requires rigorous adherence to privacy laws and safety protocols, particularly concerning data retention and model hallucination.Privacy and the India DPDP Act 2023 ComplianceBecause the platform targets learners, it will inevitably interact with minors. The Indian Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act of 2023 strictly defines anyone below the age of 18 as a child.Under Section 9 and Rule 10 of the DPDP Act, the platform must obtain verifiable parental consent before processing any personal data of a user under 18. The architecture will implement a mandatory age-gating flow; if the stated age is under 18, account activation will require a parental verification step, such as a micro-transaction or institutional authorization. Furthermore, the DPDP Act explicitly prohibits tracking, behavioral profiling, and targeted advertising directed at children. The platform's architecture inherently separates telemetry data from identity, and third-party tracking pixels (e.g., Meta or Google Analytics) will be strictly disabled for child accounts. Users and parents will also have a one-click mechanism to purge all audio recordings, transcripts, and FSRS histories from the PostgreSQL database and object storage.Security and Prompt Injection DefensesConversational LLMs are highly vulnerable to prompt injection attacks, where a user attempts to break the persona constraints (e.g., instructing the interviewer to execute arbitrary code or discuss inappropriate topics). To mitigate this, the Agent architecture utilizes a routing layer. The user's transcribed input is passed through a lightweight, local classifier (e.g., an ONNX-quantized BERT model) to detect intent anomalies before it reaches the primary Groq Llama 3.3 instance. Furthermore, the XState machine enforces strict contextual boundaries; if the user's topic deviates heavily from the scenario_id parameters, the state machine triggers a predefined fallback response, preventing the LLM from wandering off-topic.Assessment Validity and ObservabilityPedagogical trust is paramount. AI outputs must not be presented as infallible objective truth. To validate the platform's GOP and IELTS scoring, the system will be continuously benchmarked against standard datasets like speechocean762. The objective is to monitor GOP correlation with human experts, targeting a Pearson correlation greater than 0.70 at the sentence level. From a UX perspective, UI elements will explicitly label scores as "AI Estimates" and provide tooltips explaining the margin of error, ensuring users focus on the actionable corrections rather than treating the score as an official certification.11. Unresolved Questions and Recommended ExperimentsTo finalize the production architecture and ensure scalability, several hypotheses require empirical testing prior to full-scale launch.GOP Alignment-Free Efficacy in Production: While academic literature strongly supports the superiority of alignment-free CTC models for GOP in handling insertion and deletion errors, its computational overhead in a highly concurrent web environment remains untested.
