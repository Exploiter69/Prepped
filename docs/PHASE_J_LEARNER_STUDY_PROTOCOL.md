# Phase J — Optional Learner Usability Study Protocol

**Status:** Protocol prepared; no participants recruited and no study run.  
**Purpose:** formative usability only. This protocol does not evaluate placement readiness, general English proficiency, or causal learning gains.

## Research question

Can an engineering student independently find and complete a short learning or practice task, understand its instructions, use a text alternative when audio is unavailable, interpret the feedback as formative guidance, and recover from a voice failure?

## Participants and recruitment

- Optional convenience sample of 3–5 adult engineering students for an initial formative usability pass.
- Participation is voluntary and unrelated to access, grades, placements, or institutional evaluation.
- Do not recruit minors for this protocol.
- A small convenience sample can reveal usability problems but is not representative and cannot estimate population outcomes.

## Session tasks

1. Find and begin one English foundations lesson.
2. Complete one objective item and one open self-review response.
3. Find the transcript for a listening activity and complete it without audio if preferred.
4. Complete one short speaking prompt by typing; recording is optional.
5. Open one Career Studio, answer a prompt, and explain what the formative feedback does and does not claim.
6. If the participant chooses, deny microphone permission and recover using text mode.

## Data minimisation

- Do not collect raw audio, video, passwords, student IDs, grades, confidential project code, or sensitive demographic information.
- Record only anonymous task completion, observable interaction issues, participant comments, and optional broad device/browser category.
- Do not copy full answer content into research notes unless separately justified and explicitly consented; prefer short anonymised observations.
- Store notes locally outside the public repository. Never commit identifiable research notes.

## Consent and withdrawal

Before a session, explain the prototype status, tasks, optional nature, data collected, local storage location, retention period, and that participation can stop at any time without penalty. Obtain affirmative consent before taking notes. Provide a way to request deletion of that session's notes. Do not begin if consent is unclear.

## Retention and reporting

- Delete raw session notes after the agreed review period (default 30 days) unless participants explicitly agree to a different period.
- Report participant count, recruitment context, task completion, observed issues, missing data, and limitations.
- Do not describe a convenience sample as representative.
- Do not infer causal learning gains from this short uncontrolled usability study.
- A learning study requires a separate narrow skill-specific task and predeclared pre/post or delayed-recall method before data collection.

## Consent script

“I am testing an early local learning prototype, not evaluating you. Taking part is optional and will not affect your grades or opportunities. You may stop at any time. I will not record your voice or collect your student ID. I may write anonymous notes about whether tasks are understandable and where the interface causes difficulty. These notes will be stored locally and deleted after the agreed period. Do you freely agree to take part?”

Record consent separately from task notes; do not store participant names in the repository.
